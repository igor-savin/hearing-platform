# SEO Content Strategy — July 15, 2026

## Executive decision

Use the three-video framework as a controlled publishing system, not an auto-blog:

1. Create bottom-of-funnel articles around real practice services and patient decisions.
2. Strengthen local relevance through useful Westchester context and intentional links to office, service, provider, and area pages.
3. Publish a small number of timely hearing-technology explainers when the story is directly relevant to patients.
4. Keep every medical article clinically reviewed, independently sourced, and distinct in purpose.
5. Improve technical SEO and internal linking continuously.
6. Earn links through useful local resources and relationships. Do not use automated backlink exchanges.

This approach keeps the scalable parts of the video strategy while avoiding doorway pages, scaled-content abuse, unreviewed medical claims, and link-scheme risk.

## What the videos contribute

### Video 1: local service-and-location architecture

The useful idea is a service × location content map with deliberate structure, internal links, calls to action, trust signals, images, and local context. The warning in the video is also correct: changing only a city name is not enough.

Application for this site:

- Treat the existing office and area pages as local hubs.
- Link relevant articles to the nearest office, service, and area page.
- Add a service/city page only when Search Console shows demand and the practice can supply genuinely unique local information.
- Do not create dozens of near-identical “hearing test in [town]” pages.

### Video 2: a niche news desk

The useful idea is to monitor current stories inside the practice's subject area, shortlist only relevant ones, cite the original reporting or primary source, add the practice's interpretation, and route readers into evergreen service content.

Application for this site:

- Publish one or two timely explainers per month, not several rewritten news articles per day.
- Favor FDA, NIH/NIDCD, peer-reviewed research, Bluetooth SIG, CMS, and manufacturer regulatory announcements.
- Require a clear patient question and a durable “what this means for you” angle.
- Update or retire news posts when the facts or product availability change.

### Video 3: three growth pillars

The useful pillars are branded content, technical optimization, and authority. The site should use practice-specific services, locations, care pathways, review attribution, internal links, structured data, and source links. Technical checks should cover titles, descriptions, canonicals, image alternatives, internal links, Article and FAQ schema, sitemap inclusion, and indexability.

The authority component needs a safer implementation than the automated backlink pool promoted in the video. Google's spam policies explicitly identify excessive link exchanges and automated link creation as link spam. Use earned local and professional links instead.

## Local SEO plan

### Google Business Profile

- Confirm which of Harrison, Somers, White Plains, and Scarsdale are active patient-facing offices before expanding local content.
- Keep name, address, phone, hours, appointment URL, and service list consistent across the site, Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, major health directories, and professional directories.
- Post one useful update per office each month, linking to the most locally relevant article or service page.
- Add real office, clinician, equipment, and accessibility photos regularly.
- Ask for reviews through a neutral, compliant workflow; never gate reviews or offer incentives.
- Answer profile questions using language consistent with the verified service pages.

### Local landing pages

Before creating a new service/city combination, require all of the following:

- Confirmed service availability and office details.
- Search demand or impression evidence in Search Console.
- Unique travel, parking, accessibility, office, clinician, or community information.
- A distinct patient need that is not already served by the office or area page.
- Original copy, imagery, FAQs, internal links, and a clear conversion action.

Start with enhancements to active office pages rather than new URLs. The first candidate modules are hearing testing, hearing-aid repair, tinnitus care, and earwax removal at each confirmed office.

## Authority and backlink plan

Use relationship-based, editorial links:

- Complete and reconcile profiles with ASHA, the American Academy of Audiology, local chambers, hospital/provider directories, senior-resource directories, and community organizations.
- Create a physician referral resource describing red flags, referral steps, accepted records, office contacts, and what patients can expect.
- Offer expert commentary to Westchester and Fairfield County publications on safe listening, OTC devices, tinnitus, and healthy aging.
- Partner with libraries, senior centers, music schools, employers, and community groups on educational events with useful recap pages.
- Publish original local resources worth citing, such as a Westchester safe-listening venue guide or a hearing-access checklist for event organizers.

Do not buy links, join automated backlink pools, or create reciprocal partner pages solely for ranking credit.

## Measurement framework

Set up a baseline on the day each article is published and review at 28, 56, and 90 days.

Primary metrics:

- Non-branded Search Console impressions and clicks by article.
- Queries entering positions 4–20, which are the best optimization candidates.
- Clicks from articles to service, office, contact, phone, and appointment actions.
- Google Business Profile website clicks, calls, direction requests, and appointment actions by location.
- Indexed-page status and canonical selection.
- Referring domains earned to the article or its linked service page.

Secondary metrics:

- Engagement with the table of contents and related links.
- Assisted conversions where an article appears earlier in the session path.
- AI referral traffic and documented brand citations, treated as directional rather than a vanity score.

Decision rules after 90 days:

- High impressions, low click-through: rewrite the title and description to match the query more precisely.
- Positions 8–20: add expert detail, original practice insight, stronger internal links, and a relevant office/service link.
- Traffic without conversion: strengthen the patient decision section and appointment path.
- No impressions: check indexing and cannibalization, then merge, retarget, or retire rather than publishing a near-duplicate.

## Editorial safeguards

- A named audiologist should clinically review every medical article before production publication.
- Do not claim that hearing aids prevent dementia, that tinnitus has a universal cure, or that a product fits an individual without evaluation.
- Recheck time-sensitive prices, insurance information, device availability, software compatibility, and regulatory status at publication.
- Preserve meaningful publication and modified dates; never change dates only to simulate freshness.
- Use AI for research organization, drafting, and structure, while requiring human clinical review, source verification, and original practice input.
