Sudden hearing loss is an emergency.

Know the red flag  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post1_Sudden-hearing-loss-is-urgent.png

If hearing drops suddenly in one ear — over hours or a day, often noticed on waking — that is not something to watch and see. It is one of the few genuine emergencies in our field.

Sudden sensorineural hearing loss responds to prompt treatment, and the window is measured in days rather than weeks. Treated early, many people recover much or all of what they lost. Left for a month, the odds fall considerably.

The reason it gets missed is that it rarely hurts. It usually feels like a blocked ear, so people assume wax or a cold and wait it out.

If this describes you or someone at home, call our San Mateo office today on (650) 342-9449 and say it came on suddenly. We will get you assessed and referred.

Football season is back. So is 100 dB.

Hearing protection  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post2_Stadium-and-crowd-noise.png

A packed stadium runs between 90 and 110 decibels for hours at a stretch. At that level, damage is measured in minutes, not seasons — and it accumulates quietly across every game you attend.

Nobody wants to be the person in earmuffs at the game, which is exactly why filtered custom plugs exist. They are moulded to your ear, sit nearly invisibly, and drop the volume evenly instead of muffling it. You still hear the crowd, the commentary and the person next to you. You just leave without the ringing.

That post-game ringing, incidentally, is not the ear being tired. It is the ear reporting injury.

Some prescriptions affect your hearing.

Hearing health  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post3_Ototoxic-medications.png

More than 200 medications are known to affect hearing or balance. Certain antibiotics, some chemotherapy agents, loop diuretics, and high sustained doses of aspirin are the ones that come up most often.

To be clear, this is not a reason to stop anything. These drugs are prescribed because they are doing important work, and the risk is usually dose-dependent and often reversible when it is caught early. The problem is that nobody is watching.

A baseline hearing test before or early in a course of treatment gives your physician something to compare against. Without it, a change is invisible until it is substantial.

If you are starting a medication like this, call our San Mateo office on (650) 342-9449 and ask about a baseline.

The restaurant test tells you the most.

Everyday hearing  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post4_Hearing-in-noisy-restaurants.png

People often tell us their hearing is fine at home and impossible in restaurants. That combination is not a contradiction — it is the single most common early pattern we see.

Understanding speech in noise asks far more of the ear and the brain than understanding it in a quiet room. The high frequencies that carry consonants are usually the first to go, and consonants are what separate similar-sounding words. Lose those and vowels still come through, so speech sounds present but blurred, which is why people describe mumbling rather than volume.

A proper evaluation includes a word-recognition score, which measures clarity rather than loudness.

Dizziness often starts in the ear.

Balance care  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post5_Vertigo-dizziness-and-balance.png

The organ that keeps you balanced sits in the inner ear, immediately alongside the one that lets you hear. When people are surprised that an audiologist asks about dizziness, that adjacency is the reason.

Some patterns point fairly clearly. Brief spinning triggered by rolling over in bed or tipping your head back suggests BPPV, where displaced crystals disturb the balance organ — often correctable in a single visit with a positioning manoeuvre. Episodes combining vertigo, fluctuating hearing and ear fullness point elsewhere, toward Ménière's.

Falls are the real cost of leaving this alone, and they are far from inevitable.

The phone is where people give up first.

Assistive technology  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post6_Phones-and-captioned-calling.png

Long before anyone stops going to restaurants, they stop answering the phone. There is no face to read, no context to lean on, and compressed phone audio strips out exactly the frequencies that carry consonants.

So calls get handed to a spouse. Appointments get made by someone else. It looks like preference and it is really avoidance.

Captioned telephones display the other person's words on a screen as they speak, so you listen and read at once. Modern hearing aids also stream calls straight into both ears, which is a substantial improvement over holding a handset to one.

Over-the-counter fits some people.

Choosing well  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post7_OTC-versus-prescription.png

Over-the-counter hearing aids are a legitimate option, and we will say so plainly: for a healthy adult with mild to moderate loss who is comfortable self-adjusting, they can genuinely work.

Where they do not is more specific than the packaging suggests. They are not designed for severe loss, for one-sided loss, for children, or for anyone with drainage, pain, sudden change or persistent one-sided tinnitus — all of which warrant examination before amplification of any kind.

The other gap is verification. An OTC device is fitted to an average ear rather than to yours, and no measurement confirms what actually reaches your eardrum.

Bring what you bought and we will measure it honestly. San Mateo, (650) 342-9449.

Most hearing aids fail in month two.

Why fittings fail  ·  Call button  ·  CHC_Post8_Follow-up-care-and-the-drawer.png

The hearing aids that end up in a drawer rarely got there because they were faulty. They got there because the first weeks were harder than anyone warned, and there was no one to call.

Adjusting is a real process. Your brain has been running without those frequencies for years and initially finds them intrusive — running water sounds enormous, your own voice sounds strange. That settles, but it settles faster with adjustments along the way, which is exactly the stage where a mail-order purchase leaves you alone.

We build follow-ups into every fitting: verify with real-ear measurement, then refine against how life actually sounds.
