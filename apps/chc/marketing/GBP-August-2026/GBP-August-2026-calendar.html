Your ears don’t get a day off in August.

Hearing protection  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post1_Summer-noise-on-the-Peninsula.png

August on the Peninsula is loud. Outdoor concerts, a night at the ballpark, the leaf blower next door, the table saw in the garage — most of it sits well above the 85 dB line where damage starts.

Here's what makes noise exposure different from most hearing problems: it is almost entirely preventable, and almost entirely permanent once it happens. Foam plugs from the drugstore help, but they muffle everything equally, which is why so many people take them out halfway through.

Custom-fit protection is molded to your ear and filtered so speech and music stay clear while the harmful energy comes down. Musicians, hunters, contractors, and law-enforcement patients wear ours all day without fighting it.

“Live with it” is not a plan.

Tinnitus  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post2_Tinnitus-care.png

If you've been told that ringing in your ears is something you'll simply have to get used to, you were given incomplete advice.

Tinnitus is one of the most common reasons patients come to us, and it's an area we focus on. A proper tinnitus evaluation looks for the why: hearing loss driving the sound, earwax, medication effects, jaw and neck involvement, and the red flags that call for a medical referral — pulsing in time with your heartbeat, sudden onset, or tinnitus in one ear only.

From there the plan is built around you. Sound therapy, tinnitus retraining therapy, hearing aids where hearing loss is part of the picture, and practical strategies for the hours that are hardest — usually the quiet ones.

Tinnitus evaluations at our San Mateo office. Call (650) 342-9449.

The kids get a checkup in August.

Hearing testing  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post3_Back-to-school-reset.png

Peninsula schools are back in session, physicals are done, forms are signed. Most families run this checklist every August for the kids and skip it entirely for themselves.

Hearing is worth adding. Age-related loss arrives so gradually that the usual first sign isn't 'I can't hear' — it's the TV creeping louder, asking people to repeat themselves in restaurants, or losing the thread at a group dinner. On average, people wait years between noticing and doing something about it.

A comprehensive evaluation takes about an hour. You leave with an audiogram you actually understand, a word-recognition score, and a straight answer — including 'your hearing is fine, see you in two years,' which is a perfectly good result.

Quiet rooms make tinnitus louder.

August 15 · National Relaxation Day  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post4_National-Relaxation-Day-tinnitus-a.png

National Relaxation Day lands this Saturday — a fair moment to talk about the hour that isn't relaxing for a lot of our patients.

Tinnitus rarely gets louder at night. The room gets quieter. With no competing sound, your brain has nothing else to lock onto, so the ringing moves to the front of the line. Add the frustration of lying awake and the volume seems to climb further.

What tends to help: low-level background sound that sits just under the tinnitus rather than drowning it out, a consistent wind-down routine, and easing off the late-evening caffeine and screen time that keep an already-alert nervous system switched on. For many patients, hearing aids with a sound-therapy program handle the daytime and make the evening far less loaded.

San Mateo office. Call (650) 342-9449.

Hearing is how we stay in the room.

August 21 · National Senior Citizens Day  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post5_National-Senior-Citizens-Day.png

National Senior Citizens Day is this Friday, August 21. It's a good prompt for a conversation many families keep postponing.

Untreated hearing loss doesn't stay in the ears. It shows up as skipped dinners, a quieter person at the family table, more time alone at home. The 2024 Lancet Commission on dementia lists hearing loss among the largest modifiable risk factors for cognitive decline — not a guarantee of anything either way, but a strong argument against waiting and seeing.

If someone you love has been turning up the TV, dodging restaurants, or answering questions that weren't asked, the kind approach isn't to keep repeating yourself. It's to offer to come along to a hearing test.

Sometimes it isn’t hearing loss at all.

Earwax removal  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post6_Earwax-and-sudden-muffled-hearing.png

One of the most satisfying appointments we do takes about fifteen minutes. A patient arrives convinced their hearing has fallen off a cliff, and the actual problem is a plug of wax.

Earwax is supposed to be there — it protects and cleans the canal on its own schedule. It becomes a problem when it's pushed back in. Cotton swabs are the usual culprit, and hearing aids and earbuds can contribute too. Please skip ear candling; it has no evidence behind it and a real burn risk.

In-office removal under magnification is quick, controlled, and lets us look at the eardrum while we're in there. If the muffling turns out not to be wax, you're already in the right place for a proper evaluation.

Hearing technology  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post7_Hearing-aid-technology-in-2026.png

Today's hearing aids stream calls straight from your phone, recharge overnight, sort speech from restaurant noise, and in some cases sit invisibly in the canal for months at a time. We're independent, so we fit Oticon, Phonak, ReSound, Signia, Starkey, Widex, Unitron, Rexton and Lyric — and recommend by what fits your ears and your life, not by what we're supposed to move.

But here's the part that gets skipped: two people with the same device and the same audiogram can have completely different results. Ear canals differ, and a manufacturer's default settings are a starting point, not a prescription.

Real-ear measurement puts a tiny probe microphone in your ear and measures what the device is actually delivering at your eardrum, then adjusts to target. It's the difference between a hearing aid you wear and one that ends up in a drawer.

Check your benefits before October.

Insurance & benefits  ·  Call button  ·  What's new  ·  CHC_Post8_Insurance-and-Medicare-benefits-ch.png

Medicare's annual open enrollment runs October 15 through December 7. If hearing care is on your horizon, the useful work happens now — not in December when you're deciding under a deadline.

Worth knowing: Original Medicare doesn't cover hearing aids, but many Medicare Advantage plans include a hearing allowance, and the amount and the network vary a lot from plan to plan. Employer plans, union and retiree coverage, and HSA and FSA dollars may all apply. CareCredit financing is available too.

Walking into enrollment knowing what your current plan actually covers — and what your hearing looks like on paper — puts you in a much stronger position to choose well.
