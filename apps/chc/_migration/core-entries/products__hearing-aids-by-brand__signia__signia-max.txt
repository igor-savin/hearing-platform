    ["Four neural networks instead of one", "The architectural change is real, and it is worth understanding before you weigh the marketing around it. Signia says MaX runs four deep neural networks simultaneously: one for speech detection, one for own-voice detection, one for scene analysis, and one for noise processing. They are coordinated as a single adaptive system rather than four separate features taking turns. In the wearer-facing language, these surface as RealTime Conversation Enhancement AI, Own Voice Processing AI, Ambient Scene Adaptation AI and Dynamic Noise Control AI. The practical claim behind the architecture is about judgement rather than raw noise reduction. A single-network system knows how to suppress noise; it does not necessarily know whether you are in a conversation, whether the voice it just found is your own, or whether the room changed. Splitting those decisions across purpose-trained networks is meant to stop the device from doing the wrong helpful thing. Muting a restaurant while you are trying to hear the person across the table, say, or ducking your own voice as you speak. Whether that produces a better result in your ears is a separate question, and one no published evidence can yet answer."],
    ["What Signia claims, and who measured it", "MaX launched with a short list of striking numbers. We think you should see them with their sources attached rather than as bare facts. Signia reports that 97% of wearers perform better with MaX in noisy group conversations than with key competitors, and that MaX ranks first in signal-to-noise ratio performance in group conversation in noise. Both figures cite white papers authored by Jensen and colleagues in 2026. Those are Signia's own documents, not peer-reviewed publications, and not currently available to read anywhere we can find. The competitors are never named, and the sample size, test protocol and outcome measure are not disclosed. Signia reports that the new MaX chip delivers 54 times more processing power, 50% greater energy efficiency and twice the memory. The footnote attached to that sentence reads, in full, \"Compared to Signia IX.\" That is a baseline statement rather than a citation. There is no study behind it. And Signia reports that RealTime Conversation Enhancement now processes 75% more data points per second than on Signia IX, a figure that carries no footnote at all. None of this makes the claims wrong. Bench measurement and internal validation are how hearing aids get engineered, and Signia's research group has a long record. It does mean nobody outside the company has checked any of it, and that the most impressive-sounding numbers are chip specifications compared against Signia's own previous product rather than evidence about how you will hear."],
