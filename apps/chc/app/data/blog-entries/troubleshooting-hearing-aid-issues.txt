      ["No sound at all", "A hearing aid that has gone completely quiet is the most alarming symptom and often the easiest to solve. Start with power: replace the battery with a fresh one, or if it is rechargeable, set it back on the charger and confirm the charging light actually comes on. Next, look at the part that sits in your ear. A wax guard clogged with debris blocks sound as effectively as a switched-off device, and swapping that tiny white filter frequently brings a dead aid back to life. Make sure the device is turned on and turned up, and that a program hasn't been nudged into mute. If fresh power and a clean wax guard change nothing, the problem is likely internal and worth a professional look."],

Check the wax guard and the sound outlet first, and inspect the dome or earmold for buildup you can gently wipe away. Tubing on some styles traps moisture or a plug of debris that dulls everything. It is also worth checking your own ears: a canal blocked with [earwax](/services/earwax-removal/) will muffle even a perfectly working device, and no amount of cleaning the aid will fix that. If everything looks clear and sound is still thin, your hearing itself may have shifted, which is a good reason to book a [hearing check](/hearing-testing/)."],
      ["Whistling and feedback", "That thin whistle is the sound of amplified audio leaking back out of your ear and being picked up a second time. The most common reason is fit. If the device or earmold isn't seated fully, press it in gently and see if the whistle stops. Gently is the operative word, since there is [a point past which a device](/how-far-do-you-push-hearing-aid-in/) should not go, and a hearing aid you cannot get back out on your own has gone beyond it. Feedback also spikes when wax in your ear canal bounces sound back, so a canal that needs [earwax removal](/services/earwax-removal/) can be the real trigger. A dome that has loosened or is the wrong size, or tubing that has hardened with age, will do it too. Occasional whistling when you cup a hand near your ear or pull on a hat is normal; constant whistling you can't settle usually means the fit needs adjusting, which we can handle at a [fitting](/hearing-aid-fitting/) visit."],

Heavy Bluetooth streaming, whether phone calls, music, or television, pulls far more power than plain listening, so a day of streaming naturally shortens runtime. Moisture is the other big drain; humidity and sweat sap the cells and, over time, the electronics. Storing the aids in a drying container overnight and leaving the battery door open when they are off both help. With disposable batteries, letting them sit for a full minute after you peel the sticker gives them a stronger start. If a [rechargeable pair that used to last all day](/rechargeable-hearing-aid-battery-life-and-charging/) suddenly can't, the built-in battery may be aging and worth having checked."],

Different [brands](/products/hearing-aids-by-brand/) use their own apps and steps, so the exact taps vary, but the pattern is the same."],
