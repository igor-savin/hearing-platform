      ["How loud noise injures the ear", "Deep inside the cochlea sit thousands of microscopic hair cells that translate sound vibrations into signals your brain can read. Loud noise batters them. A sudden blast — a gunshot, a firecracker — can tear them mechanically, while prolonged exposure overworks them until they wither. Unlike skin or bone, these cells have no repair mechanism in humans; once a batch is gone, it is gone for good. That is why noise damage is cumulative and permanent, and why specialists treat prevention as the whole ballgame."],
      ["Everyday sources that add up", "Most people picture rock concerts and jet engines, but the quieter culprits do plenty of harm because they last so long. Prolonged time above roughly 85 decibels — heavy city traffic, a gas mower, a crowded restaurant — starts to add up. Power tools and personal earbuds are two of the biggest modern offenders. A simple rule of thumb: if you have to raise your voice to be heard by someone an arm's length away, the background is loud enough to matter."],

Persistent ringing can settle in as [tinnitus](/tinnitus/what-is-tinnitus/), which frequently travels alongside noise injury.
