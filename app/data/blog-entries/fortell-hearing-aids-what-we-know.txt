      ["The idea, in one paragraph", "Most hearing aids that advertise artificial intelligence classify your surroundings and then adjust: this is a restaurant, so reduce noise and narrow the microphones. Fortell's position is that classification is the wrong level to work at, and that the device should instead pull the individual sound sources apart and rebuild the mixture with the voices you want brought forward. That is source separation, the same family of technique behind the noise suppression on a video call, and the reason it has not appeared in hearing aids before is latency. A hearing aid that lags behind the room makes your own voice sound wrong and makes lip movement stop matching sound. Fortell's answer was to build its own chip, and its founder has told reporters the system runs at roughly ten milliseconds, which is inside the window where most wearers stop noticing. Whether or not the marketing around it holds up, this is a genuine engineering position rather than a rebrand of existing processing, and it is different from what Signia, Oticon, Phonak and Starkey are each doing with neural networks in their 2026 flagships."],

Our description of the headline figure as having changed was wrong. The ten-times claim needs the same care. It is an odds ratio rather than a count of words. In the hardest condition tested, minus 6 dB, the model puts the odds of identifying a word correctly 18.9 times higher with the Fortell device than with the control, which the company converts to roughly ten times the probability for a typical listener. The raw group averages are 50% against 7% at minus 6 dB, and 80% against 31% at 0 dB. We set the 0 dB percentages against a claim the company makes about the hardest condition, called the ratio two and a half, and have withdrawn it. What is left is a point about presentation rather than fact: a shopper reads ten times more words as ten times the words, and the figure behind it is a modelled odds ratio at the single most difficult signal-to-noise ratio in the study. That is worth asking about. One thing has not changed. The NYU researcher quoted on Fortell's homepage saying he had never seen so categorical a result also told the same reporter that the configuration used was “a configuration that’s particularly good to show the advantages of this aid”, and that different configurations might show different results.
