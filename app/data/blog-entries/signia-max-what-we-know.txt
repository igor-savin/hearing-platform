      ["The idea, in one paragraph", "Almost every hearing aid advertising artificial intelligence runs a single neural network trained to do one job: separate speech from noise. Signia's position with MaX is that one network is being asked to make too many decisions at once. Is this a conversation or background chatter? Is the voice it just found yours or somebody else's? Did the room change? A network optimised for noise reduction does not necessarily know, and when it guesses wrong it does the wrong helpful thing — muting a restaurant while you are trying to hear across the table, or ducking your own voice as you speak. So MaX splits the work across four purpose-trained networks and coordinates them. Signia calls the result Acoustic Intelligence. It is a genuine architectural position rather than marketing dressing, and it is different from the bet Oticon made two weeks earlier with Reveal, which runs two AI systems with deliberately opposing goals.
