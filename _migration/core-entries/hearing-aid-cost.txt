    ["What the evidence says the extra money does not buy", "This is the part most cost pages leave out, and it does not help us sell anything. Two trials matter here. In 2025 Wu and colleagues published a randomised comparison in JAMA Otolaryngology: 245 adults, hearing aids at roughly $4,400 against hearing aids at roughly $1,100, and no significant difference in outcome between them. Eight years earlier, Humes and colleagues reached the same conclusion in the American Journal of Audiology. Price had no measurable effect once the device was fitted well. Read that carefully, because it is easy to over-read. Neither trial says premium technology is worthless, and neither says a cheap hearing aid is as capable in a restaurant. What they say is that past a certain point, money added to the device buys you less than care put into fitting it. The 2025 trial found the other half of the same picture: a properly conducted fitting beat self-fitting by a margin researchers count as clinically meaningful. So when the choice is a top-tier device fitted quickly somewhere cheap against a mid-tier device fitted and verified carefully, take the second one.
