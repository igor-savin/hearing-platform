    ["What the technology actually is", "EON is built around a new chip Phonak calls HYPERSONIC, which replaces the two-chip DEEPSONIC arrangement used on Infinio Sphere. The headline feature running on it is Spheric Speech Clarity 3.0, a deep neural network that separates speech from background noise in real time before the rest of the processing gets to it. Alongside it runs AutoSense OS AI 8.0, Phonak's automatic program system, which classifies your surroundings, Phonak says as often as seven hundred times a second, and blends the appropriate feature set rather than making you choose a program. A third addition, WindBlock 2.0, targets the specific problem of wind striking the microphones. The genuinely new part is not any one of these. It is that the neural network now reaches further down the range. On the Infinio generation, Spheric Speech Clarity was confined to the top two technology levels; on EON it appears at the Premium 90, Advanced 70 and Standard 50 levels, and WindBlock 2.0 is available across all of them. For a practice that regularly fits people who cannot justify a top-tier device, that is a more consequential change than any of the numbers in the press release."],
    ["What Phonak claims, and who measured it", "EON launched with four striking figures. We think you should see each one with its source attached. Phonak states that the Audéo EON Sphere outperforms four other hearing aids for clear speech in loud noise; that footnote cites Ulloa Sanchez and colleagues, 2026, published as a Phonak Insight, Phonak's own in-house series. The four competitors are not named, and no protocol, sample size or outcome measure is given. Phonak states that WindBlock 2.0 reduces wind noise by up to 50 dB and can improve the signal-to-noise ratio by up to 30 dB; the footnote defines the comparison as WindBlock 2.0 switched off, and the supporting preference study, by Vaisberg and colleagues, ran with nineteen experienced wearers. And Phonak states that EON uses 37% less power, is 25% smaller and 20% lighter; those footnotes read, in full, that the comparison is with Phonak Infinio Ultra running the previous version of the same feature, and with the previous Sphere generation. Those are baselines, not studies. One footnote on Phonak's own product page says simply that a claim is based on Sonova internal data and invites you to email the company if you want to know more. None of this means Phonak is wrong. Internal validation and bench measurement are how hearing aids get built, and Phonak's research group is a serious one with a long publication record.
