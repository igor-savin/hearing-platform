    ["What actually changed", "The processor. Infinio Sphere used a two-chip arrangement, with a dedicated DEEPSONIC coprocessor handling the neural network alongside the main chip. EON replaces both with a single processor Phonak calls HYPERSONIC. The version of the neural network it runs is Spheric Speech Clarity 3.0, up from 2.0 on Infinio Ultra. Around it, AutoSense OS AI 8.0 replaces version 7.0 as the automatic program system, and WindBlock 2.0 is added to handle wind across the microphones. The physical consequence of the new chip is the part you can see and feel: Phonak states EON is 25% smaller and 20% lighter than the previous Sphere generation, and uses 37% less power while running the neural network. Both figures are footnoted as comparisons against Infinio Ultra rather than as study results, which is the appropriate way to state them. They are engineering specifications, and engineering specifications are the kind of claim a manufacturer is actually in a position to make. On the connectivity side, EON adds Auracast broadcast audio, joined by scanning a QR code in the myPhonak app, and it works from launch day rather than being promised in a later update. RogerDirect, Bluetooth Classic and LE Audio carry over."],
    ["The numbers, and what stands behind them", "Phonak's headline claim for EON is that wearers are twice as likely to understand every word from any direction. If you are considering this as an upgrade, you should know what that number is measuring. Its footnote cites a 2024 Phonak Field Study News by Wright and colleagues, and defines the comparison as hearing aids using Spheric Speech Clarity against the same hearing aids with Spheric Speech Clarity switched off. Your Infinio Sphere already has Spheric Speech Clarity switched on. That claim describes a benefit you already have. The claim that the Audéo EON Sphere outperforms four other hearing aids for clear speech in loud noise comes from a 2026 Phonak Insight by Ulloa Sanchez and colleagues, Phonak's own in-house publication series, and the four competitors are never identified. Nothing in either document is a comparison of EON against Infinio Ultra. The battery figures are worth reading carefully too. Phonak quotes up to 56 hours for Infinio Ultra Sphere and up to 38 hours for EON, which looks like a step backwards until you read the footnote, which is identical on both: the maximum depends on your hearing loss, how noisy your environment is, how much you stream and which features are actually switched on. Running the neural network was the single largest drain on the previous platform, and reducing that cost is the entire purpose of the new chip. The two headline numbers are not describing the same thing, and comparing them directly will mislead you in both directions."],
